Removing F alone was not enough, and finding out why mattered. The board cannot stay 7x7: clue
slots are min(rows, cols) and there is a hard invariant that slots equal the piece count, one row
per person. Six pieces on 7x7 gives seven slots against six pieces, which clue_slots asserts
directly, and the UI would build a seventh card with no content behind it. So the board shrinks to
6x6 as well.

The shrink is minimal and provably safe. The region split is unchanged, still v,*,4 and h,4,* as
in case_002, with the seventh row and column simply gone; objects lose their out-of-range second
cells. The solution needed no coordinate changes at all, because V(5,6) and E(6,5) already fit,
and the resulting zones come out exactly as designed: R_T four pieces, R_V two, R_C zero, zero
hard-constraint violations, submit passes. The capacity arithmetic closes too -- R_V holds exactly
V and E against a capacity of 2.
